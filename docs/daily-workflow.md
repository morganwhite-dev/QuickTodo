# Daily workflow

This is the routine I use. It's built from real QuickToDo features. Try it for a week, then change whatever doesn't fit you.

[Back to the README](../README.md)

## The idea

When I'm overwhelmed, the problem isn't the work. The problem is the sorting. So I split the day into small jobs. Capture is fast and mindless. Sorting happens later, in one short block. Doing starts with one task, not a list of 40.

## Step 1: Capture the moment it shows up

Don't hold a thought in your head. Put it in right away.

- Press Control, Option, Command and Space in any app.
- Or click the QuickToDo icon in the menu bar.
- Or click **+ Capture** in the app's top bar.

Type it the way you'd say it. Example: `email advisor thursday 10am #School`. If you don't have a date or list, type only the words. The task lands in General, and you can sort it later.

## Step 2: Start with Next Move

Open the **Decision Board**. The amber Next Move card shows one task. An overdue task comes first. If nothing is overdue, the soonest due task wins.

Click **Start** to move it to In Progress. That's the whole decision. You don't have to choose from the full list.

If the task feels too big, open it and look at the Next Move suggestion. It may say Break Into Steps. Add a few checklist steps and start with the first one. On Macs that support Apple Intelligence, the editor can suggest steps for you.

## Step 3: Sort General once a day

Open **General**. It holds tasks with no due date, in High, Medium, and Low lanes.

For each task, pick one:

- Drag it to a priority lane.
- Give it a due date, and it moves to the board.
- Right click it and choose **File as Someday** if it can wait.

This takes a few minutes. You don't need to finish the whole pile.

## Step 4: Plan the week

Open **Upcoming**. You see a 7 day strip. Click a day to see its timeline from 8 AM to midnight.

Check these things:

- **Free time blocks.** Gaps of 30 minutes or more show as free time.
- **The week panel.** It shows tasks, booked time, how many are done, and your busiest day.
- **Time by list.** A bar shows how your planned minutes split across your lists.

If one day is packed, right click a task and choose **Move to Tomorrow**. You can also drag cards on the Decision Board.

Coming later: I plan to add an import to calendar feature. Then your calendar events will sit next to your tasks, and the free time blocks will reflect real plans. It is not available yet.

## Step 5: Protect personal time

I don't want a plan that is only obligations. These features help me keep time for what I enjoy.

1. **Make a Personal list.** Type `#Personal` in Quick Add. QuickToDo creates the list the first time you use it. You can recolor it in Manage Categories.
2. **Plan fun like a real task.** Add `hike saturday 10am #Personal`. Give it an estimate in the task editor, such as 2 hours. Then it takes up real space on your timeline.
3. **Use free time blocks.** In Upcoming, click **Plan this time** on a free block. Quick Capture opens with that time already set. Type what you want to do.
4. **Repeat what matters.** In the editor, turn on **Repeats** for things like a weekly game night or a daily walk. You need a due date first.
5. **Check the balance.** In Upcoming, look at **Time by list**. If Personal is empty, add something.
6. **Mark your focus hours.** In Settings, turn on the **Focus Window** and set your hours. Use that time for hard work. Keep the rest of the day lighter.

## Step 6: Close the day

Open **Today**. The ring shows how many tasks you finished. Finish what's left, or move it to a day with room. Anything you completed by mistake can be undone from the short Undo prompt or from the Completed tab.

## A sample day

| When | What I do | Where |
|------|-----------|-------|
| All day | Capture every thought as it comes | Shortcut or menu bar |
| Morning | Start the Next Move task | Decision Board |
| Midday | Sort new tasks | General |
| Evening | Check what's left and what's tomorrow | Today and Upcoming |
| Sunday | Plan the week and book personal time | Upcoming |
